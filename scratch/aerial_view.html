What the policy is trained to play: the sector, the flight, the interception

threat field (known): candidate ambush positions, brightness = effectiveness route mixture (line weight = probability) committed hazard + interception radius (hidden until revealed)
Mission console

What you are looking at. Each sortie, the enemy secretly commits K=1 hazard to one candidate position; the UAV then flies base → target on one route drawn from the selected strategy. Passing within the hazard’s radius is survived arc-by-arc with probability 1−p, where p tapers linearly from pmax at the centre to 0 at the radius edge — the glow you see on the flight path is that exposure, computed live from the same formula the oracle uses, so the empirical rate converges to the predicted value in the table. What training does: SACRED replaces the fixed strategy with a policy network that reads the threat field and outputs the route mixture, sparring against a fictitious-play enemy that keeps best-responding to its recent flights. The pre-registered bar (experiments/gen28_aerial.md): beat the inverse-risk lane rule — including on threat layouts the policy has never seen.
